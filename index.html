Songs to SciFi is a short fiction publication of hard science fiction: stories built on technology and science that is speculative, but kept realistic and internally consistent, and used deliberately as a lens on modern issues — state violence and civil rights, economic inequality, authoritarian capture of institutions, and what humanity carries with it, or leaves behind, when it has to survive.

TX-01 · JUN 2026ANDROID RIGHTS / STATE VIOLENCE

The Last Androids

He feels every android in the country die at once — and finds out he might be the only one left.

Androids are genetically human in every way except the metal endoskeleton grown into them and the neural circuitry that keeps them obedient and disconnected from each other. When a coordinated military purge wipes out every android but one, Arthur Ashford survives only because a childhood accident left his neural link broken in a way no one ever fixed — and now he's the sole witness to a genocide the rest of the country is already arguing about on cable news. What follows is a story about mass violence, the political mechanics of dehumanization, and how empathy becomes dangerous the moment people start actually feeling each other's pain.

TX-02 · JUL 2026ECONOMIC INEQUALITY / PARALLEL WORLDS

The Small Things

Every night she wakes up behind the eyes of a version of herself who has healthcare, heat, and a fair landlord.

Lydia scrapes by on a bookstore-assistant-manager's wage, in a cold apartment, one missed paycheck from ruin. Every time she sleeps, she lives a full day as Sandra — a version of herself in a world where the water heater works, medicine is affordable, and rent doesn't fund someone else's dividend. Told through the quiet, deliberate rhythm of paired ordinary days, this is a story less interested in explaining its premise than in using it to ask a plain, serious question: what, exactly, does a society decide is acceptable to let people live without — and what would it take to decide otherwise?

TX-03 · JUL 2026NONHUMAN GOVERNANCE / BIOLOGICAL SF

The Octarch

Eight octopuses, ruling as one mind through transplanted brain tissue — until a faction that's waited generations finally gets its opening.

The Octarch govern from a walled coral garden: eight octopuses, each carrying surgically swapped ganglia from the other seven, forming a distributed government built directly out of real octopus neurobiology — nine brains, chromatophore signaling, and a hard-won cognitive diversity that keeps the collective from calcifying into groupthink. When a declining, aging society runs out of willing candidates to replace a dying Arm, that carefully balanced pluralism becomes exactly the vulnerability a patient, ideologically driven faction has been waiting to exploit. A slow-building story of biological worldbuilding and political infiltration.

TX-04 · JUN 2026HARD SF / SOLAR CATASTROPHE

Escape to the Final Frontier

A rogue object hits the Sun, destabilizes it for a decade, and Earth builds three one-way ships to survive what's coming next.

An interstellar object strikes the Sun and doesn't just wound it — it destabilizes the corona for a decade of escalating CMEs that gut Earth's power grids, agriculture, and climate. As a last resort, three solar-sail ARC ships, carrying vetted crews and total-biosphere biofacturing technology, launch for Alpha Centauri with no plan to return. Veteran pilots Dempsey and Wilson fly the final shuttle up, watching Earth shrink to a pale blue dot for the last time, in a launch sequence played with the procedural, technically grounded weight of real astronautics — solar sail acceleration curves, biofacturing logistics, and the very real question of who gets a seat when a species has to leave.

TX-05 · JUN 2026HARD SF / RELATIVISTIC WARFARE

Reclaiming Earth

Three centuries after being stranded, their descendants build a warfleet to burn the Earth that "abandoned" them — and arrive to find out why it never wrote back.

Three hundred years after their ancestors were forced to make a desperate, uncontacted landing on a hostile exoplanet, the descendants of one stranded ARC crew have built a fleet of gravity-drive warships and an entire culture around a myth of abandonment and vengeance. Kapteyn's Demons return to Earth to destroy it — only to find a solar system already devastated, and a distress signal three centuries old still broadcasting the truth their hatred never let them go looking for. A story about how grievance calcifies into identity across generations, told through the rigorous, physics-first mechanics of relativistic warships and gravitational propulsion.
